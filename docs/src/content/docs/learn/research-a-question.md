Use this lesson when you want the papers or pages to stay with the answer. You ask one question. The reply is a report, and each claim keeps a source you can open later.

<aside class="dt-prereq">
<p class="dt-prereq-title">Before you begin</p>
<p>Open the app and connect one language model. If this computer does not have that yet, start from the <a href="/get-started/desktop/">desktop quickstart</a>.</p>
</aside>

![Choose Research and type a cited question](/gifs/learn-research-a-question.gif)

The clip opens **Mode**, chooses **Research**, and types the question. **Research settings** opens beside the composer. The clip stops before you send.

## 1. Open chat in the project workspace

Open [Chat](/learning-workspace/chat/) in the project workspace.

You are in that workspace's chat.

## 2. Choose Research

Open **Mode** and choose **Research**. Research looks up the question and returns a report with citations.

The message shows **Research**, and **Research settings** opens beside the composer.

## 3. Decide where the sources come from

Attach a knowledge base when the sources should come from your library. Leave it off, and this turn uses the web and preprint search the workspace already allows.

After you attach one, its name shows on the composer.

## 4. Ask one question

Send: “What changed in retrieval-augmented generation after 2023? Cite the papers you use, and stop at six.”

The question is in the thread.

## 5. Wait for the report

Wait until the report is in the thread.

The report includes citations.

## 6. Open one cited source

Open one cited item before you rely on a claim.

The source opens. This lesson ends when the report is in the thread and you have opened one of its sources.
