Move one project across its workspace, Chat, Learning, and Resources. The next session starts from the work you saved. Partners and Personalization stay on your account.

## Two scopes, five shelves

| Shelf | Scope | What you use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Chat](/learning-workspace/chat/) | Workspace | Conversations, a task board, and your Learning notebook |
| [Books](/learning-workspace/books/) | Workspace | Books, Mastery Paths, Reading collections, and Practice |
| [Resources](/learning-workspace/resources/) | Workspace | Chat history, Favorites, question bank, learning journal, and knowledge bases |
| [Partners](/channels/) | Account | Persistent companions, their instructions, resource access, and channels |
| [Personalization](/customize/) | Account | Personas, skills, connected agents, MCP, CLI apps, and memory controls |

A skill is a reusable method you can attach to a turn. MCP is a connection that lets DeepTutor call a tool outside the app.

**Calendar is an exception within Home:** it shows an account-wide view across workspaces. [Calendar](/learning-workspace/calendar/) explains how to read which workspace an event came from. A Partner also has its own working context. Living on your account does not mean every conversation can read every workspace.

## 1. Give a project its own workspace

Create or select a workspace for “Signals and sampling.” Keep its lecture notes and exercises separate from another project such as “Academic writing.” Before you add material or start a study activity, check the name of the active workspace. After you switch, Chat, Books, and Resources show that project. Your Partner list and Personalization library stay available. See [scope and switching](/learning-workspace/workspaces/).

## 2. Bring in sources, then ask a focused question

Add material you want to reuse to [Knowledge Center](/learning-workspace/knowledge/). When you want the page open beside the chat, open that document in [Reading](/learning-workspace/reading/). Back on Home, attach the source you mean, then ask something concrete: “Explain why low sampling rates can make two different signals look identical; cite this primer.”

A file you saved earlier is not included in the next question on its own. Attach it, or select the learning context the activity offers. Before you build on the answer, read the passages it cites.

## 3. Separate evidence from your own understanding

Save passages you may want again in [Favorites](/learning-workspace/favorites/). Write what you think they mean in a [Learning notebook](/learning-workspace/notebook/), and insert a reference where it helps. The notebook is your explanation. Favorites keeps the original material. When a claim looks wrong, reopen the source. When a paragraph needs work, ask Dito next to that paragraph.

## 4. Choose the learning activity that fits

- Use [Books](/learning-workspace/books/) for a sequence of pages and interactive blocks.
- Use [Mastery Path](/learning-workspace/mastery/) when the goal splits into topics you study in order.
- Use [Reading](/learning-workspace/reading/) when the question is about a document or a moment in the media.
- Use [Practice](/learning-workspace/questions/) to answer, read the feedback, and come back to the parts you missed.

These are different ways to work on one topic. You do not have to finish them in this order. Start with one. Use the capture or attachment control on that screen to carry the result into the next activity.

## 5. Reuse assistance without mixing projects

Pick a persona that asks you to explain your reasoning. Add a skill when you want the same method again. Connect an external agent when you want a second opinion, or set up a Partner with a role and a limit on which resources it can see. Those account items are still there in the next workspace. Each workspace keeps its own materials and study records.

## Return to the work

On Home, use the [Task Board](/learning-workspace/task-board/) for what to do next. Open [Resources](/learning-workspace/resources/) for saved evidence and past chats. Reopen an activity from the Learning list. Switching subjects does not mean setting the app up again, or creating the Partner a second time.
