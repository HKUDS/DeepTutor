Open **Home → Calendar** (`/calendar`) to see dates and study time together. **Calendars and events belong to the account**, even though their entry is under Home. Workspace layers add the selected workspace's tasks, due reviews, scheduled instructions and activity footprints. Switching workspaces changes those layers while keeping your personal events.

![Calendar](/screenshots/dev/calendar.png)

## Add a study event

1. Open a date or time in the calendar and create an event.
2. Enter a title, start and end, and choose whether it is an all-day event.
3. Choose its calendar and optional location, notes, repeat rule and reminder times.
4. Save it, then reopen it to inspect or adjust details. For a repeating event, choose whether a change affects this occurrence, following occurrences or the series where supported.

Use separate calendars and colors to distinguish classes, personal commitments and study blocks. Month, time-grid and agenda views help you work at different scales. A reminder setting records the desired reminder time; delivery depends on the available notification/runtime setup.

## Read the workspace layers

| Layer | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Tasks | Due dates from the workspace's Task Board, with linked time blocks where present |
| Reviews | Mastery and Practice reviews due on a date |
| Automations | Scheduled chat instructions and their expected run times |
| Footprints | Past conversations, practice reviews and mastery evidence |

Toggle layers to reduce visual clutter. A scheduled instruction is different from an event: it asks the assistant to run later in its conversation. A due review still needs you to open the relevant learning activity and complete it.

## Plan one week

Keep your classes in an account calendar. In the `Probability` workspace, add a task due Friday and reserve a study event on Wednesday. Review the due-learning layer before choosing that slot. When you switch to another workspace, Wednesday's event remains visible while the task and study layers change to that project's context.
