The **Learning notebook** is your writing and thinking space within a workspace. Open **Home → Learning notebook** at `/co-writer`; this is the route historically called **Co-Writer**. The current editor uses pages and blocks, with Dito beside the page, rather than the former Markdown source/preview interface.

![A Learning notebook page and Dito](/screenshots/dev/notebook-detail.png)

## Create and organize pages

1. Choose **New page** for a topic, lecture or paper, or use **Import Word** to bring in a `.docx`.
2. Name the page and write your first blocks. Use the page tree to create subpages and move between them.
3. Pin pages you frequently revisit. Search on the notebook home reaches pages and subpages by title and preview text.
4. Check the save status in the page header before leaving. If saving fails, resolve the error rather than treating the visible draft as safely stored.

Pages can have icons, covers and parent pages. A large subject can become a top-level page with separate subpages for definitions, proofs and exercises. Deleting a parent moves its subpages up one level; deleting the page itself needs confirmation.

## Ask Dito at the place you are working

Select a passage or focus a block and open Dito. Try **Explain this**, **Rewrite**, **Make shorter**, **Give an example**, **Translate**, or **Quiz me on this**. An empty line can become a starting point for new writing. The conversation stays associated with the page and the line or block it concerns; the page's conversation list lets you reopen it later.

A reply can be inserted below the line for review or saved as a subpage. Proposed edits appear as suggestions: review, accept, dismiss or discuss them. If the target block has changed, your edits are preserved and the stale proposal needs a fresh discussion. This is useful when you want AI help without losing track of which changes you adopted.

![A page-linked conversation with Dito](/screenshots/dev/notebook-dito.png)

## Bring sources into your own writing

Use **Insert favorite** to search, preview and insert original records from [Favorites](/learning-workspace/favorites/). You can also ask Dito to synthesize several favorites with citations. Source insertion and knowledge-base actions help ground notes in material you can revisit.

For example, create `Conditional probability`, insert a saved explanation, then write your own counterexample. Ask Dito to check the argument and propose a clearer paragraph; review the suggestion before applying it. Keep a `Practice` subpage for the questions the topic raises.

## Study inside the page

The notebook can hold learning blocks such as quizzes, flashcards, diagrams and other supported generated content. Ask Dito for a learning activity at the relevant line, inspect the generated block, and use the block's menu when you want another version. Generation depends on the required capability and model being available. A saved block is part of the page; revisiting it is different from requesting a new version.

## Export and reuse

The page menu exports **Markdown** or **Word**. Word keeps text and source links; interactive blocks become static. Review the export when you need to submit or share it. Keep Favorites for original source records and the notebook for your synthesis, structure and study activity.

Notebook pages belong to the active workspace. Switching workspaces changes the page library. [Personalization](/customize/) can shape how the assistant helps across workspaces, while the page content remains with its project.
