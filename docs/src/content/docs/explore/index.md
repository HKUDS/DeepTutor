The sidebar is a map of **where your work belongs**. Choose a workspace first, then move between its conversations, learning activities, and reusable sources. Your account supplies the companions and preferences you can bring into that work.

## Two scopes, five shelves

| Shelf | Scope | What you use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Chat](/explore/chat-workspace/) | Workspace | Conversations, a task board, and your Learning notebook |
| [Learning](/explore/learning/) | Workspace | Books, Mastery Paths, Reading collections, and Practice |
| [Resources](/explore/space/) | Workspace | Chat history, Favorites, question bank, learning journal, and knowledge bases |
| [Partners](/explore/partners/) | Account | Persistent companions, their instructions, resource access, and channels |
| [Personalization](/explore/personalization/) | Account | Personas, skills, connected agents, MCP, CLI apps, and memory controls |

**Calendar is an exception within Home:** it offers an account-wide view across workspaces. [Calendar](/explore/calendar/) explains how to read the source workspace of an event. A Partner also has an explicit working context: its account-level identity does not mean every conversation can read every workspace.

## 1. Give a project its own workspace

Create or select a workspace for “Signals and sampling.” Keep its lecture notes and exercises separate from another project such as “Academic writing.” Before adding material or starting a learning activity, check the active workspace. Switching changes the first three shelves’ contents, while your Partner and Personalization libraries remain available. See [scope and switching](/explore/workspaces/).

## 2. Bring in sources, then ask a focused question

Add reusable material to [Knowledge Center](/explore/knowledge/), or open a specific document in [Reading](/explore/reading/) when you want to work beside the source. In Home, attach the relevant context and ask something concrete: “Explain why low sampling rates can make two different signals look identical; cite this primer.”

A stored asset is not automatically included in every model request. Attach sources or select the supported learning context explicitly. Check cited passages before building on an answer.

## 3. Separate evidence from your own understanding

Save useful original passages to [Favorites](/explore/favorites/). Then write your interpretation in a [Learning notebook](/explore/co-writer/), inserting references where useful. The notebook is where you develop an explanation; Favorites preserves the material you may want to reuse. Reopen the source when a claim needs checking, and use Dito beside a paragraph when you need help improving it.

## 4. Choose the learning activity that fits

- Use [Books](/explore/book/) for a structured sequence of pages and interactive blocks.
- Use [Mastery Path](/explore/mastery-path/) for a goal broken into topics and guided study.
- Use [Reading](/explore/reading/) for questions tied to a document or media timestamp.
- Use [Practice](/explore/practice/) to answer, inspect feedback, and revisit weak areas.

These are different ways to work with a topic, not steps you must complete in order. Start with one and use the supported capture or attachment controls to connect it to the next.

## 5. Reuse assistance without mixing projects

Choose a persona that asks you to explain your reasoning. Add a relevant skill when you need a repeatable method. Connect an external agent for consultation, or configure a Partner with a particular role and resource scope. Those account assets remain available in another workspace, while each workspace keeps its own learning records and materials.

## Return to the work

Use Home’s [Task Board](/explore/task-board/) for next actions, [Resources](/explore/space/) for saved evidence and history, and the Learning list to reopen an activity. You do not need to repeat setup or recreate a Partner whenever you switch subjects.
