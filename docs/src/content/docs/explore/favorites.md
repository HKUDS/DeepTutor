**Favorites** stores material you want to keep: useful conversations, explanations, research results and other supported learning outputs. Open **Resources → Favorites** (`/notebooks`), create or choose a collection, and reopen the saved records inside it.

Favorites and the **Learning notebook** have different roles. Favorites keeps source records; the notebook at `/co-writer` is where you write and organize your own pages. Some saving controls and older integrations still use the name **Notebook** for a Favorites collection.

![Favorites preserve original source records](/screenshots/dev/favorites.png)

## Save something worth keeping

1. In a supported conversation or result, use its save or bookmark action.
2. Choose a destination collection, or create a collection for a topic such as `Fourier — worked examples`.
3. Open Favorites to check the stored title, content and source information.
4. Organize records by collection. Move or copy records when your topic structure changes, or export a collection as Markdown.

A favorite is saved material rather than a new AI request. Opening a record does not start generation or change the source archive. Presentations vary with content: conversations retain speaker structure, questions can reveal the answer, articles show a contents list when long enough, and supported media can be played from the record.

## Use favorites in a notebook page

1. Open a [Learning notebook](/explore/co-writer/) page.
2. Choose **Insert favorite** from the page menu.
3. Search **All favorites**, or narrow to a collection and content type.
4. Preview records and select one or several. The insertion order follows the order you selected them.
5. Insert the records, or ask Dito to organize the selected sources into a coherent note with source citations.

Inserted favorite blocks retain saved content and a link back to the favorite record. Treat them as source snapshots: write your own interpretation alongside them rather than assuming they track every later change to the original conversation. A stable favorite link continues to identify the saved record after a move.

## A useful collection structure

For one module, keep three collections: `Sources`, `Worked examples`, and `Questions to revisit`. Save a clear explanation from Chat, a useful research result and a reading note. In the notebook, place the relevant favorites next to your own summary, then ask Dito for practice questions based on that page.

Favorites belong to your current workspace's resources. They do not automatically become a knowledge-base index or global memory. Attach saved material in workflows that offer it, insert it into a notebook page, or use it as an explicit source when creating supported learning activities.
